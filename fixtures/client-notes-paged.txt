Client: NorthPeak Outdoor Gear (fictional demo company)
Internal notes from account team, Q3 review — full notes across departments

SECTION 1: CUSTOMER RETENTION
Logo retention dropped from 92% to 84% over the last two quarters. The decline
is concentrated almost entirely in customers acquired through the paid social
media channel in the last 6 months — retention for customers from organic
search and referral channels has stayed flat at 93%. Support team flagged a
40% increase in tickets mentioning "shipping delays" starting in June,
coinciding with the switch to a new fulfillment warehouse partner. No pricing
changes have been made in the last 12 months. Customer success has not yet
run an exit survey for churned accounts this quarter, so we do not have
direct reasons from customers who left. Account managers report anecdotally
that several long-time wholesale partners have also mentioned slower restock
times since the warehouse transition, though none have formally churned.
The customer success team's working theory is that new customers, who have
not yet built loyalty or trust with the brand, are far less tolerant of a
single bad delivery experience than repeat customers who have a track record
of reliable service to fall back on when judging whether one bad experience
is an anomaly or a pattern.

SECTION 2: IT SYSTEMS MIGRATION
The company completed a migration from an on-premise email server to a
cloud-hosted provider in May. IT reported minimal downtime (under two hours)
during the cutover weekend. A handful of employees needed help resetting
two-factor authentication afterward, but no data loss was reported. The next
planned IT project is upgrading the point-of-sale terminals in the three
physical retail locations, tentatively scheduled for next spring. IT has also
proposed consolidating three separate internal ticketing tools (used by
support, facilities, and engineering respectively) into a single shared
platform, which would simplify reporting but requires budget approval and a
multi-week data migration effort that has not yet been scheduled.

SECTION 3: OFFICE LEASE RENEWAL
The lease on the downtown warehouse office space expires in fourteen months.
Facilities has requested quotes from three commercial real estate brokers to
evaluate whether to renew in place, downsize given more staff working
remotely, or relocate closer to the new fulfillment partner's distribution
hub. No decision is expected before the next fiscal year planning cycle.
Preliminary broker feedback suggests downsizing could save roughly 15-20% on
annual lease costs given current commercial vacancy rates in the area, but
would require reconfiguring the current open-plan layout to fit a smaller
footprint, which facilities estimates would cost around two months of rent
savings to execute.

SECTION 4: MARKETING CAMPAIGN BUDGET
The Q3 marketing budget was split roughly 55% paid social, 25% search ads,
and 20% affiliate/referral partnerships. Leadership approved a 10% budget
increase for Q4 to support the holiday season, with the increase allocated
primarily to paid social given its historically strong new-customer volume,
though a decision on channel mix for the increase is still pending review.
The marketing team also ran a limited test of influencer partnerships in
August, which drove a modest bump in site traffic but conversion tracking
was inconclusive due to a tagging issue that has since been fixed for future
campaigns.

SECTION 5: EXECUTIVE TRAVEL POLICY
HR updated the corporate travel policy to require manager pre-approval for
any single trip exceeding $1,500, down from the previous $2,500 threshold.
The change was prompted by a routine finance audit recommendation rather
than any specific incident, and takes effect at the start of next quarter.
HR also plans to roll out an updated expense reporting tool alongside the
policy change, replacing the current spreadsheet-based process, with training
sessions for managers scheduled over the following month.

SECTION 6: WAREHOUSE PARTNER TRANSITION DETAILS
The switch to the new fulfillment warehouse partner took effect in early
June. The prior partner's contract was not renewed due to recurring capacity
constraints during peak periods in the previous holiday season. The new
partner offered lower per-unit fulfillment costs and additional warehouse
capacity, but the transition required migrating inventory records and
integrating a new shipping API, which operations has acknowledged had a
rougher-than-expected rollout in the first several weeks, including the
delay issues referenced by customer support.
